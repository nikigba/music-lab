How far from home

Gold is home (C). Distance counts how many sharps or flats separate C major from the chord's home key. A minor chord's home key is its relative major, so A minor lives in C and E minor lives in G. Minor chords add half a step and diminished chords add a full step, so you can tell them apart from their major partner. Each key's diminished chord is the one built on its leading tone (B° in C, F♯° in G), which is why it sits in the same column.

The farther away, the cooler and dimmer the color. The sharp side (G, D, A…) cools through amber, coral and mauve. The flat side (F, B♭, E♭…) cools through lime, sage and teal. Both meet in blue at F♯, the far side of the circle.

The chips under the status line show three chords the triad lives inside, all taken from the triad's own key: as the 1–3–5, the 3–5–7 and the 5–7–9. Tap one to hear its bass note go under the triad. The drone fades out while it plays, because a C drone under another bass note clashes.

With “Voice-lead” on, each triad starts on its note closest to the last note you heard, so moving between triads sounds like one line instead of jumping back to the root. The other two notes alternate direction: one triad goes up from that note, the next comes down. Press Esc to start fresh from root position. The quiz always plays root position, so the voicing can't give the answer away.

Tap a minor triad and it's treated as the ii of a ii–V. Every triad that works over its V7 lights up, and the V itself gets a “V” tag. A triad works if all three of its notes fit one of the usual dominant scales (mixolydian, altered, half-whole diminished, lydian ♭7, whole tone). Bright rings hold the V7's 3rd or 7th, which pull home. Faint rings fit but float: they hold the 11th (a sus sound) or neither guide tone.

“Guess the color” plays a hidden triad. Tap the card you think it was. Try to answer from the color you hear, not by naming the chord.
