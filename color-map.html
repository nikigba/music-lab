How it works

Gold is home (the tonic). The farther a chord is from home, the more its color cools and dims, ending in blue at the tritone. Distance is the number of steps around the circle of fifths to the chord's root, plus half a step for each note outside the major scale. Sharp side cools through amber, coral and mauve; flat side through lime, sage and teal. Colors and numbers stay the same in every key; only the pitch moves.

Routes decide where each triad starts after the last note you heard. Closest note starts on the nearest chord tone and plays the other two in random order. Alternating does the same but switches direction each chord, up then down. Step up and Step down force the next triad to start just above or just below the last note, so the line keeps climbing or falling until it hits the edge of the piano range and turns back. Esc starts fresh from root position.

Looping a few bars: drag across bars (or tap one, then Shift-click another) to select them. Space or the play button loops just those bars, ignoring the Loop checkbox. Click anywhere outside the selection to stop and clear it; the control panel doesn't count, so you can change tempo or route while it loops.
