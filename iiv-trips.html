ii–V Triad Trips

Press play. A trip is a triad on the ii chord in one shape, a triad on the V entered by its nearest note, then landing home by step. Each trip repeats, note for note, until it's in your ear, with the chords playing again after each one so you can sing it back. Then one thing changes. Real licks with the same two triads come at the end of each pair.

How it works
Home: the C drone holds home quietly under every trip, from the moment you press play. If you like, each new pair can start with the home triad instead.
ii: one triad over Dm7, a little away from home (F keeps the 1; Dm keeps nothing).
V: one triad over G7, farther away (B° and F° hold 7 and 4, the notes that most want to go home).
Home: the nearest home note, usually a half or whole step from the last note, then the C triad.
Your turn: the chords again with no melody. Sing the trip back, or play it.
Real lick: a ii–V–I from your solos or Stitt's licks whose ii and V bars outline the same triads, moved to C, in its real rhythm.
