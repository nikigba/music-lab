Pick a triad over Dm7 on the left. The triads over G7 it voice-leads to light up on the right. Every card plays just its own triad, so you can go back and forth. Numbers are measured from C, the tonic.

Reading the glow

Strong: moves 1–2 semitones onto a note that pulls home Soft: smooth but sus, or a step longer Same notes, only the bass moves

B and F are the guide tones of G7 (its 3rd and 7th). The classic 2–5 sound is C, the 7th of Dm7, falling a half step to B, while F holds. Triads marked sus contain C, the 11th of G7, so they float instead of pulling home.

With “Voice-lead from last note” on, Dm7 triads always play the same way, root up (so F always ends on 1). Each G7 triad then starts on its note closest to that last note, and the other two follow in random order; the number that's sounding lights up on the card. So after F ends on 1, B♭ starts on 2. Press Esc to start fresh from root position.

Keyboard: press a number to play the triad that starts on it (the first number on each card). The first press picks from Dm7, the next from G7, and so on. On the G7 side press − before the number for a flat root (−2 D♭, −3 E♭, −6 A♭m, −7 B♭) or + for the major version (+3 E, +6 A). Esc starts over on the Dm7 side. With keys, the G7 triad always starts on the number you pressed (no voice-leading); the other two notes still come in random order.

The altered triads borrow notes from outside C major. Each one names its tensions over G7: D♭ is the tritone sub (♯11, ♭7, ♭9), A♭m and E♭ come from the altered scale, E and B♭ from the half-whole diminished scale, A from lydian dominant. Their flat and sharp notes (D♭, E♭, G♭, A♭) sit a half step from C, E or G, so they pull home too. Turn on “Resolve to C” to hear where each one lands.

The diatonic triads on both sides are the same seven C-major triads. Only the bass changes what they mean: Am is the 5–7–9 of Dm7 but the 9–11–13 of G7. “Core stacks only” keeps 1–3–5, 3–5–7 and 5–7–9 on each side.
